Sewage Backup Causes and Prevention in Fishers, IN

Home / Blog / Why Sewage Backups Happen in Fishers, IN

For a homeowner, a backed-up sewer line ranks near the top of the list of unpleasant emergencies. Raw sewage entering your living spaces creates immediate health hazards, causes extensive property damage, and requires professional cleanup that can cost thousands of dollars. Understanding why sewage backups occur and taking preventive measures can significantly reduce your risk. This guide from Fishers Restoration covers the common causes of sewage backups in Fishers, IN and practical steps you can take to protect your property.

What Causes a Sewer to Back Up

When something keeps wastewater from draining to the city sewer or your septic tank as designed, it comes back into the building. Most cases trace back to the following: sewer pipes that have aged or collapsed, roots from trees intruding into the pipe, grease, hair, and debris clogging the line, heavy rainfall overloading the municipal sewers, a combined sewer overflow event, or improper items going down the toilet, including excessive paper, wipes, and feminine products.

Tree Root Intrusion

Roots from nearby trees are a leading reason sewer lines in Fishers get blocked. A sewer pipe is a steady supply of moisture, which is exactly what roots go looking for. They work their way in at cracks, joints, and connection points, then slowly fill the inside of the pipe until it blocks and backs up. The risk is highest with aging clay tile pipe, which roots penetrate readily. A camera inspection of the sewer line every 2 to 3 years can catch roots early, before a backup happens.

Ways to Lower Your Backup Risk

To guard a Fishers house against backups, start by having a backwater valve fitted to your sewer connection so the city system cannot flow back in. Keep cooking oil and grease out of every drain. Only flush toilet paper, never wipes, feminine products, or other items. Schedule regular sewer line inspections and cleaning. Address slow drains promptly before they become complete blockages. Find out where your sewer cleanout is so it can be reached in an emergency. Think about adding a sewage backup endorsement to your homeowner's policy, or buying sewer line insurance.

Sewage Came Up? Do This First

If you experience a sewage backup, do not attempt cleanup yourself. Sewage contains dangerous pathogens including bacteria, viruses, and parasites that can cause serious illness.
