How Drying Works on an Evansville Water Damage Job

A wet building left alone does dry eventually, but far too slowly. Moisture inside walls, studs and subfloors can linger for weeks, and that is plenty of time for mold to grow and wood to distort. Our job on every Evansville property is to speed that up in a controlled way.

First the liquid water comes out, because extraction removes far more water per hour than evaporation ever will. Next, air movers placed around the room drive air over damp materials so the water in them evaporates. Dehumidifiers pull that moisture out of the air and send it down a drain, which keeps the room dry enough for evaporation to continue. Without the dehumidifiers, the air movers would just move damp air around.

A technician measures the wet materials daily against readings from an unaffected area of the property. Most homes in Evansville reach that dry standard in 3 to 5 days, with dense materials like hardwood or plaster taking longer. We remove the machines when the readings reach the goal, and not before.

What Usually Goes Wrong in Evansville Homes

Some water problems need a phone call this minute, and others can safely wait until morning. We have ordered these five common Evansville calls by how fast each one needs attention.

Bursts: Nothing is more time-critical than a broken water line, which keeps pouring until the supply is cut. Cold weather, depending on the season, is one trigger, with the leak appearing as the frozen section thaws. Close the main valve, open a faucet to relieve pressure and call right away, even at night. Ceilings that bulge with trapped water should be left alone and the room below kept empty.

Runoff: Storm water coming under a door or through a garage is next in line. There is no valve to close here, so lift what you can off the floor and avoid wet electrical outlets. Having passed over pavement and soil on the way in, it carries dirt and germs. As soon as it is safe to enter, we pump the water out and take away porous items it ruined.

Kitchens: Leaks from a dishwasher, an ice maker line or the plumbing under the kitchen sink should be dealt with the day you find them. Turning off the supply valve for that appliance normally ends the leak itself. What remains is the water trapped below the cabinets and floor covering, and it will not dry by itself. For Evansville kitchen jobs, our crew can frequently save the cabinets by pushing dry air in at the toe kick.

Crawlspaces: Moisture under a raised floor is seldom an emergency, but it should not be put off for a season. Puddles on the ground cover, dripping ductwork or wet insulation between the joists are what an inspection usually turns up. Left alone, the damp air rises into the rooms above and the wood framing begins to decay. We extract the water, dry the wood to a safe reading and tell you what we think the source is.

Odors: Last on the list is a stale, earthy odor in a room that once had a leak, which is worth a scheduled visit. Since growth can start 24 to 48 hours after materials get wet, an old leak that was never fully dried is a likely culprit. Do not scrub or spray a large patch yourself, because disturbing it spreads spores through the house. Our technicians test the area, contain it and remove the growth along with the moisture feeding it across Evansville.
