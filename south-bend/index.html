How Drying Works on a South Bend Water Damage Job

Drying a building is not the same as waiting for it to dry. Left alone, water trapped in drywall, wood framing and subfloor can stay for weeks, which is long enough for mold and warping. Our job on every South Bend property is to speed that up in a controlled way.

First the liquid water comes out, because extraction removes far more water per hour than evaporation ever will. Next, air movers placed around the room drive air over damp materials so the water in them evaporates. That vapor is collected by dehumidifiers and pumped away, keeping the air dry so the process does not stall. Air movers on their own only stir humid air, so the two machines work as a pair.

A technician measures the wet materials daily against readings from an unaffected area of the property. Most homes in South Bend reach that dry standard in 3 to 5 days, with dense materials like hardwood or plaster taking longer. The equipment comes out when the numbers say so, not when the calendar does.

Typical Water Damage Problems in South Bend

Not every water problem announces itself with a flooded floor. Below are the situations our crews are called to most often around South Bend, and what each one tends to involve.

Hidden leaks: A pinhole in a supply line or a loose drain fitting can drip inside a wall for months. Watch for a water bill that climbs for no reason, soft spots on the floor, and paint that blisters. Our technicians trace the wet area with moisture meters and thermal cameras, without tearing out more wall than needed. Left alone, constant dampness rots framing and feeds mold, so a minor drip is still worth fixing now.

Sewage: When a main drain line clogs, wastewater comes back up through floor drains, tubs and toilets on the lowest level. It is typically caused by roots in the line, built-up grease or a municipal sewer overwhelmed during a downpour. This is Category 3 water, which carries bacteria and should not be handled without protective gear. Carpet, padding and other absorbent materials that got wet usually cannot be saved.

Storms: During a heavy storm, runoff may collect along the foundation faster than drains can carry it off. It enters at door thresholds, low windows and the garage, wherever the ground slopes toward the building. Because surface water carries dirt and chemicals from the ground it passed over, it is handled as unsanitary. A big enough storm can cause it at any time of year, and South Bend properties are no exception.

Crawlspace: The space beneath a raised floor is easy to forget, which lets dampness go unchecked for years. Humidity stays high down there when a pipe drips, the ground cover is damaged or water pools after rain. You may notice cupped floorboards, a stale smell in the living area or insulation hanging loose. We pump out what has collected, dry the joists and subfloor, and show you where the moisture is coming from.

Condensate: An air conditioner pulls gallons of water out of the air on a humid day. That water should leave through a small drain line, which clogs easily with algae and dust. When it backs up, the pan overflows into the ceiling, closet or hallway around the air handler. Most owners learn of it from a ceiling stain or drooping drywall, so have the drain line cleared once a year.
