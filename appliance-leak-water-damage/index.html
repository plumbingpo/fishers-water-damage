Appliance Leak Water Damage Help in Fishers

Most of the appliance leaks we see trace back to three machines. Washer supply hoses can burst and keep spraying at full pressure until the valve is closed. Dishwashers usually leak a little at a time, at the drain hose or door seal, hidden beneath the cabinets. A refrigerator ice maker line is a thin tube that can drip behind the unit for months. Each one needs a different kind of cleanup.

For homeowners in Fishers, we begin by sliding the machine out to see how far the water traveled. Kitchen leaks tend to end up beneath the cabinets and underneath the floor. With a laundry leak we look at the rooms next to it and the wall behind the washer, plus the ceiling below when the laundry sits on an upper floor. We extract what is standing, measure what has soaked in, and set drying equipment sized to the wet area.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Shut the Machine DownIf the outlet and the floor next to it are both dry, end the cycle and take the plug out of the wall. If not, switch it off at the breaker instead.
  2. Shut Off the Water LineFor a washing machine, close both the cold and hot valves on the wall at its back. Look below the sink for the valve that feeds a dishwasher. A refrigerator line has a small valve below or behind the unit.
  3. Mop Up What You CanMop or towel up what you can reach so it stops spreading under cabinets and flooring. Vacuums are safe for this only when built for wet and dry pickup.
  4. Keep the Door ClosedDo not open a washer or dishwasher that is still full. Opening the door mid-cycle can release the whole load of water onto the floor.
  5. Document What FailedKeep the part that failed, whether a hose, a fitting or a valve, and get photos of it. Both the appliance maker and your insurer may want to look at it.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • Installation mistakes like a loose supply line connection or a dishwasher that does not sit level.
  • A washer drain hose that slips out of the standpipe, or a clogged standpipe.
  • Aging rubber supply hoses on a washing machine, which weaken and then split under pressure.
  • An overflow caused by an overloaded drum or by using too much detergent.
  • Plastic or copper ice maker lines that kink, crack or loosen at the fitting.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Adjoining Rooms

For hours, a failed washer hose can keep releasing water. It passes under walls into hallways, closets and bedrooms, and soaks carpet and pad well away from the laundry room.

Wall Bases and Trim

On a flooded kitchen or laundry floor, the baseboards and the lower edge of the drywall draw water upward. Often the worst of it is on the wall in back of the appliance, where it went unseen.

Floors and What Is Under Them

Water slides beneath laminate, vinyl plank and hardwood. The boards swell or cup, while the subfloor below remains wet well after the top looks dry.

Mold Behind Appliances

Behind a refrigerator or a dishwasher it is warm and dark, and nobody checks there often. Mold on the floor and wall can be fed by a slow drip in that spot well before it is noticed.

Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

When a machine overflows unexpectedly or a hose bursts, that is typically treated as sudden and accidental, which many homeowners policies cover. Payment typically goes toward the damage the water did, and the appliance or the part that failed is not included. A claim is harder when an ice maker line or dishwasher seeped slowly, since coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks. Certain insurers will want to know the hose's age and whether it was kept up. Before cleanup, take photos, save the failed part and read your policy.

Cost

How the Quote Is Built

  • Removal of cabinets or flooring, compared with drying them in place when that is possible.
  • Duration of the leak, particularly a hidden drip that went unnoticed behind the machine.
  • Presence of mold, which adds removal work in front of the drying.
  • Spread of the water into other rooms, and into the ceiling beneath an upstairs laundry if there is one.
